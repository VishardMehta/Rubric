Rubric

Functional Requirements and Technical Architecture

AI hiring system · MVP specification · Version 1.0 · 14 August 2026

1Overview

Rubric converts a job description into an explicit scoring rubric, then evaluates every applicant against that rubric and nothing else. Applicants submit a resume and a two minute voice introduction. Both are scored criterion by criterion, with every point traceable to a verbatim quote from a named source. Shortlisted candidates complete an adaptive voice interview whose questions are planned for rubric coverage and worded from what the candidate actually said.

The design principle throughout: the model never emits a bare total. It emits per-criterion sub-scores that are summed and verified in code before any row is written. A single holistic number drifts 15 to 20 points between runs on identical input; anchored sub-scores hold steady. Every score in the product is reproducible and explainable.

2Scope

In scope

  • Job creation and automatic rubric generation
  • Resume upload with text extraction
  • In-browser voice introduction capture
  • Dual-source screening against the rubric
  • Ranked HR dashboard and candidate review
  • Manual HR approval gate
  • Adaptive voice interview, 5 to 10 questions
  • Interview evaluation and result review
  • Nine screens, two distinct user experiences

Out of scope future modules

  • Webcam proctoring, face and gaze detection
  • Anti-cheat and integrity scoring
  • GitHub and LinkedIn verification
  • PDF report generation
  • Automated email delivery
  • HR authentication and multi-user access
  • Deployment, hosting, custom domain
  • Rubric editing by hand

3Functional requirements

3.1 Job definition and rubric

IDRequirement
FR-1.1HR creates a job with title, description, required skills and experience level.
FR-1.2The system generates a rubric of 4 to 7 named criteria with integer point allocations summing to exactly 100.
FR-1.3A rubric whose points do not total 100 is regenerated with the violation stated, never silently corrected.
FR-1.4The generated rubric is displayed to HR immediately on creation, with per-criterion descriptions.
FR-1.5HR can regenerate a rubric, with a warning if applicants already exist.
FR-1.6Each job exposes a copyable public application link.

3.2 Application and screening

IDRequirement
FR-2.1A candidate submits name, email, a resume PDF up to 5 MB, and a voice introduction recorded in the browser.
FR-2.2Resume text is extracted locally. An image-only PDF is rejected with instructions rather than accepted as empty text.
FR-2.3The introduction is transcribed by a hosted model, falling back to a local model on rate limit, server error or timeout.
FR-2.4Resume text and transcript are scored against the same rubric in a single evaluation.
FR-2.5Each criterion receives points and a list of verbatim evidence quotes, each tagged as originating from the resume or the introduction.
FR-2.6Sub-scores are verified in code to sum to the reported total. A mismatch fails validation.
FR-2.7Every evidence quote is verified to appear in the source it names. A quote attributed to the resume that exists only in the transcript fails validation.
FR-2.8Discrepancies between resume and introduction are recorded and shown to HR as neutral observations. They carry no score penalty.
FR-2.9Matched and unevidenced skills are listed separately. Unevidenced means neither source mentioned it, not that the candidate lacks it.
FR-2.10A recommendation band is computed server-side and returned with the score. The frontend never derives a band from a number.

3.3 Review and approval

IDRequirement
FR-3.1HR sees all applicants for a job ranked by screening score, with recommendation and pipeline status.
FR-3.2The list can be filtered by recommendation band.
FR-3.3Candidate detail shows the score, the per-criterion breakdown with source-tagged evidence, the resume, the transcript, and audio playback.
FR-3.4Approving a candidate mints a unique, opaque, non-guessable interview token and produces a copyable link.
FR-3.5Rejecting a candidate requires explicit confirmation.
FR-3.6A failed screening can be retried without the candidate reapplying.

3.4 Adaptive interview

IDRequirement
FR-4.1The interview is reachable only by its token and only once, before completion.
FR-4.2A question plan is generated before the first question: 5 to 10 slots, every rubric criterion covered at least once, difficulty progressing from opening to deep.
FR-4.3The first three slots have fixed intents: background, projects, personal contribution.
FR-4.4Questions are spoken aloud by the browser and remain visible as text throughout.
FR-4.5Recording starts automatically once the question is delivered. The candidate is never asked to press record.
FR-4.6Each answer is transcribed and scored against its slot's criteria as it arrives, not deferred to the end.
FR-4.7Each subsequent question is generated from carried interview state, targeting an uncovered criterion and anchored on a specific claim the candidate made.
FR-4.8A question substantially similar to any earlier question is regenerated before being asked.
FR-4.9Processing displays the real backend stage. No percentage bars, no timer-driven fake progress.
FR-4.10Refreshing mid-interview resumes at the correct question. Answers are persisted per turn.
FR-4.11An answer that fails transcription can be re-recorded in the same slot without restarting.
FR-4.12The candidate is never shown a score, band, recommendation or evaluation.

3.5 Evaluation

IDRequirement
FR-5.1Technical, communication and experience sub-scores are computed in code from accumulated per-answer scores. The model does not invent them.
FR-5.2The overall score is a weighted average, verified within tolerance before being written.
FR-5.3Strengths and concerns each reference something the candidate actually said.
FR-5.4HR sees the full transcript with the rubric criteria each question probed and the response time per answer.

Frontend

React with Vite, plain CSS over a token layer. Two shells that share tokens but nothing else.

HR shellSidebar application. Dense tables, scannable, repeat use.
Candidate shellCentered, no navigation. Single-use, one task at a time.
InterviewFull viewport, no chrome at all. One control on screen.

Notable components. VoiceRecorder wraps native MediaRecorder and handles both Chrome webm/opus and Safari mp4 explicitly. AudioLevelMeter is driven by a real AnalyserNode, never a simulated waveform, because it is the candidate's only proof the microphone is working. FileDropzone validates type and size before upload. ScoreBreakdown and EvidenceList render per-criterion points with their source-tagged quotes.

Text to speech uses the browser's own speechSynthesis. It costs nothing, needs no key, and has no network round trip, so questions begin immediately rather than after an API call.

Backend

FastAPI with Pydantic v2. Fully synchronous: no queue, no workers, no background tasks. Screening holds the request open for 8 to 20 seconds, which is correct at this scale and removes an entire class of failure.

llm.pyStructured Gemini calls, retry with violation stated
stt.pyHosted transcription, local fallback
resume.pyPDF extraction and normalisation
validation.pyPost-generation checks for every stage
interview.pyPlan, state object, turn advancement
scoring.pyBand computation, weighted aggregation
heuristics.pyEvery threshold as a named constant
cassettes.pyOffline replay for demonstrations

Every numeric threshold in the system lives in one file. No literal with meaning appears anywhere else.

Three private storage buckets hold resumes, introduction audio and answer audio. The database stores object paths, never URLs; the API resolves a path to a signed URL at response time. Row level security stays enabled and the backend bypasses it with a service role key held only in the server environment.

6AI pipeline

Five calls, each returning a validated schema. No free-text parsing anywhere in the system.

StageInputOutputValidated on
RubricJob description, skills, experience4 to 7 criteria with pointsPoints total exactly 100
ScreeningRubric, resume text, transcriptSub-scores, evidence, conflicts, recommendationSum matches total; every quote found in its named source
PlanRubric, screening, transcript5 to 10 planned slotsEvery criterion covered; depth progresses
TurnAnswer, plan slot, interview stateAnswer score, claims, next questionQuestion not similar to any prior; criteria exist
EvaluationAccumulated scores, transcriptStrengths, concerns, recommendationOverall within tolerance of weighted average

The interview state object

Carried into every question generation call: questions already asked, answers given, topics discussed, claims made, criteria covered, criteria remaining, and depth reached per topic. The plan guarantees the interview covers the whole rubric. The state guarantees each question is specific. Without the plan, an interview follows whatever the candidate mentioned first and leaves most criteria unprobed. Without the state, follow-ups are generic. Together they produce "how did you handle the cold-start problem in that recommender" instead of "tell me about your experience with system design."

7Non-functional requirements

AreaRequirement
CostFree service tiers only, including during development. No paid API call at any point.
DeploymentLocalhost only. No hosting, no domain.
ReliabilityAn offline replay mode reproduces a full demonstration from recorded responses, verified with the network disconnected.
ConsistencyA test harness scores one fixture five times and asserts score range stays under threshold. Variance is the only measurable property where no ground truth exists.
Interview qualityA synthetic candidate plays through a full interview and a second pass grades the interviewer on repetition, rubric coverage, anchoring and depth progression.
AccessibilitySemantic HTML, visible focus throughout, keyboard navigable, no colour-only signals, reduced-motion respected, usable at 375 px.
PrivacyAudio and resumes in private buckets behind signed URLs. Candidates see no scores. Academic demonstration; not for screening real applicants.
ConcurrencyVerified for 5 to 10 simultaneous candidates. No claim is made beyond that.

15 to 17 working days. Work is ordered to retire risk before building surface: the AI stages and the audio pipeline are built and verified from the command line first. A complete end-to-end flow runs from a terminal at roughly day six; the first visual demonstration lands around day nine.

Two gates are absolute. Frontend work does not begin until the score consistency harness passes, because a polished interface over drifting scores is worth less than a plain one over stable scores. Nothing is handed over until offline replay has been verified with the network physically disconnected.
